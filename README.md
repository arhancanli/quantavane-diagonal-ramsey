# R(k,k) ≤ (3.4827 + o(1))^k, unconditionally, and off-diagonal bounds for R(k, γk), 1/5 ≤ γ ≤ 1

**Status: verified by two independent programs and an internal referee; not yet peer reviewed.**

Author: Arhan Canli (Quantavane, arhan@quantavane.com). On the use of AI, see
[Authorship and the role of AI](#authorship-and-the-role-of-ai).

## The results

All logarithms are natural. R(k, l) is the two-colour Ramsey number.

**Theorem 2U.** There is a constant C ≥ 1 such that for every k ≥ 1

    R(k,k) ≤ ⌈C e^{z k}⌉,   z = 311946616731/250000000000 = 1.247786466924,   e^z ≤ 3.4826255125.

So R(k,k) ≤ (3.4827 + o(1))^k. The previous best upper bound we know of is 3.49135655^k (EvolvingPrograms, 1 November
2026; see below); in the exponent z the improvement is 2.504·10⁻³.

**Theorem 1U.** The same with z = 1248056263241/10¹² (e^z ≤ 3.4835652388), from a certificate that uses no Lemma D
control (a shorter chain of proof).

**Theorem O (off-diagonal).** For an explicit piecewise-affine function Φ on [1/5, 1] (1,601 cells, given exactly by
`research/tier1/ramsey-offdiag/cert/offdiag_cd3_v1.json`), R(k, l) ≤ ⌈C e^{k Φ(l/k)}⌉ for all k, l ≥ 1 with
l/k ∈ [1/5, 1] (and, by symmetry, l/k ∈ [1, 5]). For example e^{Φ(1/2)} ≤ 2.3571884, e^{Φ(3/4)} ≤ 2.9323705 and
e^{Φ(9/10)} ≤ 3.2664794. From γ = 201/1000 to 1, Φ is below every upper bound we could derive from the published
literature and public certificates (read as generously as we could), by 2.8·10⁻³ to 6.5·10⁻³ in the exponent; below
the bound of Gupta, Ndiaye, Norin and Wei (arXiv:2407.19026, Theorem 1) by 0.015 to 0.062. At γ = 1/5 we make no
claim (Lu and Wang's source is lower there by 1.1·10⁻⁵). Table: `research/tier1/ramsey-offdiag/PROOF.md`, Section 0.

The constant C is not effective: it comes from compactness arguments in the proof.

## What this rests on, and the earlier work it builds on

The method is that of Zhao, Booth-Clibborn and Schwahn, and every bound here is built on their work and on that of
Lu and Wang:

- **ZBS**: Zhipu Zhao, William Booth-Clibborn and Casper Schwahn, *A (3.49...)^k bound for diagonal Ramsey numbers*,
  [github.com/w2ll2am/diagonal-ramsey](https://github.com/w2ll2am/diagonal-ramsey) at commit c2875f33 (8 October 2026),
  R(k,k) ≤ 3.4986238^k, with a Lean development. Their framework (the local step, the finite-host closure, banks of
  profiles, Theorem 5.7 and Corollary 5.10) is the framework of our proof, and their bank P0r12 is part of our
  certificates. Their preprint is not refereed, and their README says it was generated by AI agents.
- **Lu–Wang**: Zhipeng Lu and Sichen Wang, *Retained-Set Descent for Diagonal Ramsey Numbers*,
  [arXiv:2609.14525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14525), with data at
  [github.com/sichen-wang/diagonal-ramsey-numbers](https://github.com/sichen-wang/diagonal-ramsey-numbers) (commit
  5ec98f47). Their source function f is the source of our bank, and their Appendices A–B are the base of our source
  bound.
- **EvolvingPrograms**: [github.com/EvolvingPrograms/DiagonalRamsey](https://github.com/EvolvingPrograms/DiagonalRamsey)
  (commit 959f46b0, 1 November 2026), R(k,k) ≤ 3.49135655^k, the previous best bound we know of. Our certificates use
  no object or result of theirs; our control certificates (Lemma C) bound the same kind of integral in a way close to
  their Section 5.
- **CGMS**: M. Campos, S. Griffiths, R. Morris and J. Sahasrabudhe, *An exponential improvement for diagonal Ramsey*,
  [arXiv:2303.09521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.09521) (Annals of Mathematics), the first exponential improvement on
  4^k, whose book algorithm started this line of work.
- **GNNW**: P. Gupta, N. Ndiaye, S. Norin and L. Wei, *Optimizing the CGMS upper bound on Ramsey numbers*,
  [arXiv:2407.19026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19026), R(k,k) ≤ 3.8^k and the off-diagonal bounds we compare with;
  Lu and Wang adapt their candidate induction.
- Also compared with: the off-diagonal certificate of Neisler, Shin and Sukhatankar
  ([github.com/wamlat/RamseyLean-bootstrap](https://github.com/wamlat/RamseyLean-bootstrap), e53b8cf11d), and the
  Erdős–Szekeres bound (1935), which the proof uses.

**Unconditional.** The proof document `papers/diagonal-ramsey-2026/SELF_CONTAINED.md` (v5) restates in our own words
and proves the parts of ZBS (their Sections 3–5) and of Lu–Wang (their Appendices A–B) that we use (Sections 14–17), and
re-verifies with our own programs every object of ZBS's bank that our certificates use (Section 18). Nothing proved by
ZBS or by Lu–Wang is assumed; their data files enter only as data. What is new here is: Lemma C (a finite certificate
for a control), Theorem A and Lemma D (a weaker hypothesis for the closure step, with a finite certificate), the
extended bank, and Corollary O with the certificate of Φ. The chain is stated in Section 13.2 of that document.

## Checking it: one command

    FLINT_PYTHON=/path/to/python-with-python-flint-0.9.0 sh check.sh        # about 20-30 minutes
    FLINT_PYTHON=... sh check.sh full                                       # about 2-3 hours

`check.sh` fetches the public inputs (ZBS's tables and lines at c2875f33, Lu–Wang's source at 5ec98f47) and checks
their SHA-256; parses ZBS's tables; unpacks the two full bank certificates in `certs/` and checks their SHA-256; runs
the preflight (pins of the frozen Lemma D code, the structure, and the proviso of Theorem 5.7); runs checker B on both
banks; checks Theorem O with checkers A and B and refuses its eleven red controls. `full` adds checker A on both banks,
the 1,128 lines of ZBS's list (two programs), Lu–Wang's source criterion on 73,302 cells (two programs) and the third
Lemma D checker. It ends with `ALL CHECKS PASSED` or stops at the first failure.

- **Certificates.** `certs/certCD3-full.json.gz` (Theorem 2U; 382 MB unpacked, SHA-256 32bcafd3…07af8) and
  `certs/certK3-full.json.gz` (Theorem 1U; 363 MB, SHA-256 38091eed…ee468): our new objects plus every object of ZBS's
  bank in the reference cone, each control with a Lemma C or Lemma D certificate, the root a complementary pair at
  z. `research/tier1/ramsey-offdiag/cert/`: the certificates of Φ.
- **Checker A** (`research/tier2/ramsey-zbs/code/checkA/`): exact rationals and our own outward-rounded dyadic
  intervals. **Checker B** (`code/checkB/`): Arb balls (python-flint 0.9.0), written separately. Each implements the
  bank conditions clause by clause; both accept both banks with the same z. The Lemma D certificates are also checked
  by a third, separately written checker (`research/tier2/ramsey-lemma/cert_independent/checkD_C.py`).
- **Red controls.** Tampered certificates must be refused: 11 for the bank checkers (run in the lab; logs in
  `papers/diagonal-ramsey-2026/unconditional/cone/`), 11 for Theorem O (run by `check.sh`), preflight tampers.
- **Recorded runs.** Logs of every run on 19 November 2026 are next to the programs (`*.log`, `RESULT_*.md`).
- Rebuilding the certificates from scratch (hours, about 60 GB of disk): `research/tier2/ramsey-zbs/reproduce.sh` and
  `papers/diagonal-ramsey-2026/unconditional/cone/run_cone.sh` (paths inside are the lab's; see the proof document,
  Section 9).

## Internal review

Every block of the proof had a fresh-context internal referee and a re-referee; the code of the new programs had a
separate audit; the merged document had a further referee (all PASS or PASS WITH FIXES, fixes applied, no error
found). The reports are included: `papers/diagonal-ramsey-2026/REFEREE*.md`, `REREFEREE_20261119.md`,
`RESPONSES_20261119.md`, `papers/diagonal-ramsey-2026/unconditional/REFEREE_*.md` and `REREFEREE_BLOCKS_20261119.md`,
`research/tier2/ramsey-zbs/REFEREE_*.md`. These referees are AI agents working for Quantavane, not outside
mathematicians: this is not peer review.

## Also here: Proposition QR (a limit of the method)

`research/tier1/diagonal-next/PROOF_QR.md` proves that no local-step statement of the form used here (ZBS's Theorem
3.4, Lemma 4.5, our Lemma 4.5', with these moves and this potential) can certify a control below the quasi-random
level a_rand = −log(p(1 − μ)^w). Internal referee PASS WITH FIXES, re-referee PASS (`REFEREE_QR_20261119.md`). It is
a statement about the method, not a bound on Ramsey numbers.
